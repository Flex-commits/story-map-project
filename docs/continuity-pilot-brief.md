# A focused investigation for HOMES OS

**Working question:** after a housing placement changes, how is a student's transportation arrangement confirmed so they can continue attending school?

This is a proposed investigation, not a finding that this process is broken or that software is the answer.

## What to establish

- Interview students/families and frontline staff with the existing consent approach. Participation and publication consent must remain separate.
- Document the actual steps, responsible roles, handoffs, information requirements, waiting periods, current systems and escalation paths. Do not assume every placement follows one workflow.
- Identify who owns the operational budget, who could procure a tool, who uses it, and whether an existing system could meet the need.
- Distinguish information/follow-up delays from capacity, eligibility, placement supply, policy and funding constraints.
- Establish a baseline using authorized records: for example, elapsed time between a placement-change notification and confirmed transportation arrangements. Define both timestamps, exclusions, missing events and how records are deduplicated.
- Agree on a feasible pilot outcome and measure unintended effects. Changes in attendance require their own population, period and confounding analysis; faster processing alone does not prove improved education outcomes.

## Pilot hypothesis to test

If a documented handoff repeatedly lacks clear ownership or confirmation, a shared status and escalation workflow might reduce time to confirmation. First test that workflow with staff and families using a simple prototype. Only pursue a software product if the problem recurs, the buyer has a reason to switch, and a pilot demonstrates useful improvement.

**Reviewable deliverable:** consented workflow evidence, a specific budget-owner role, a baseline definition, and a pilot design. No buyer, delay, or outcome is asserted as established. No messages or invitations have been sent.
