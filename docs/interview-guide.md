# Listening to students, families, and staff

Prepared for Thomas Hagos. No interviews or outreach have been conducted for this project.

## Approach

Start with adult college students, adult caregivers, and staff who support them. Ask people to describe a process, not prove that they experienced homelessness. Participation should be voluntary, with no effect on services, benefits, grades, or housing. For research involving children or recruitment through Penn or a service organization, check the applicable institutional research and permission requirements before recruitment.

## Opening explanation

“I’m Thomas. I’m working on a project about housing stability and education in New York City. I’d like to understand what it takes to find support and continue school. You can skip any question or stop at any point. You do not need to share addresses, case numbers, immigration information, or anything you would rather keep private. I am not providing services or making decisions about assistance.”

Ask separately for permission to take notes, record, publish a quote, and identify the speaker. Agree on how they want to be described. Do not record without explicit consent. Obtain contact information only if needed for follow-up, keep it separate from interview notes, and agree on a deletion date. Explain any practical limits to confidentiality before starting. Do not promise anonymity if it cannot be maintained.

## Students and caregivers

1. What would you like me to understand about trying to continue school while dealing with housing uncertainty?
2. Can you walk me through a recent attempt to get housing or school-related help, without sharing identifying details?
3. How did you find out whom to contact? What happened next?
4. Which requirements, travel, schedules, or waiting periods made it harder?
5. Was there someone who helped you follow through? What did they do?
6. What happened to school attendance, studying, or daily routines, if you want to discuss that?
7. What change would have made the biggest difference?
8. What have I missed, or misunderstood?

## Staff

1. Walk through a recent de-identified request for housing support.
2. How are responsibility, next steps, and follow-up recorded?
3. What can you resolve directly, and what depends on housing supply, eligibility, funding, or another agency?
4. How do you know whether a student actually receives help?
5. What records or evidence support the problem you describe?
6. What would you change first, and why?

## Before publishing

Offer participants the chance to check their attributed words and identifying description before publication. Remove details that could expose a residence or identify another person. Label published secondary accounts as published accounts, not interviews conducted for this project. State that an individual story cannot establish prevalence or causation. Never invent dialogue, merge people into an unlabeled composite, or present a fictional journey as a reported one.
