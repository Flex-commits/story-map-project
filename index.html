01 / Establish the baseline

Before the headline,
there is a place.

This map shows students identified as homeless by each of NYC’s 32 geographic school districts during 2021–22.

Darker districts report larger counts. These are counts, not rates: a larger district may report more students simply because it serves more students.

The chapter begins in 2021–22. Changing the year updates the map only.

The records describe education agencies. They do not locate individual students or their homes.

02 / Two school years later

Two years later,
the map deepens.

The map now shows 2023–24, using the same color thresholds as the baseline so the two years can be compared.

For example, District 24 in Queens reported 11,702 students, compared with 7,388 in 2021–22.

This dataset does not distinguish the reasons for an increase. It cannot tell us how much reflects housing conditions, enrollment changes, student movement, or changes in identification.

03 / A different view

The same city.
A different lens.

Here, color represents the percentage change in the reported count between the first and last school years.

+131%change in District 30’s reported count

District 30 in Queens rose from 3,684 to 8,512, about a 131% increase. A fast-growing count is a signal to investigate, not an explanation of what happened.

A 131% increase does not mean 131% of students experienced homelessness. Enrollment-based rates require another dataset.

04 / Look closer · District 4

Come closer.
East Harlem.

4,351students reported in 2023–24

District 4 reported 1,961 students in 2021–22, 3,045 in 2022–23, and 4,351 in 2023–24—an increase of about 122% from the first year.

These records make the district a useful place to begin asking questions about family housing stability. They do not show individual families’ housing journeys or whether they received support.

06 / Queensborough · Read the evidence

One campus.
More than one kind of need.

4%reported homelessness
38%reported housing insecurity

Queensborough’s 2024 report distinguishes a homelessness question from the broader housing insecurity measure. Both describe survey experience, not a headcount of students living on campus.

The survey had 474 respondents and a 12% response rate. Results were weighted; individual question sample sizes may differ. The reported homelessness percentage was 2% in 2022 and 4% in 2024. No significance test is provided here.

LaGuardia’s separate report gives a 5% homelessness figure, with 747 respondents and a 14.9% response rate. These small differences should not be read as a campus ranking.

07 / From evidence to investigation

A referral is
a beginning.

What happens between needing help and receiving it?

The next stage of this project will investigate how families navigate housing support while keeping children connected to school.

  • Which housing and school-support tasks become hardest during a move?
  • Who takes responsibility when a task remains unresolved?
  • Where could a practical change help—and where is more housing or funding needed?

These are research questions. This workbook does not establish that service coordination failures caused student homelessness.

08 / Read the evidence

Every mark
has a source.

Counts: supplied NYSED SIRS workbook, SED-SIRS-3-Year-Summary-2022-2024.xlsx, “3-yr Homeless Counts” worksheet. Original publication link remains to be verified.

Boundaries: NYC Department of City Planning, School Districts, release 26b, downloaded October 24, 2026. Historical boundary consistency has not yet been verified.

The map excludes separate charter records, District 75, and the central office. Students can be counted by multiple education agencies. Do not sum these records into a unique citywide total or add years into a count of unique people.

Suppressed values are treated as unavailable, never zero. No enrollment rates, home locations, or service outcomes are shown.

College locations: New York State, CUNY University Campus Locations, downloaded October 24, 2026. All 26 source records are retained; this is not a map of every NYC university or every CUNY building.

College surveys: Queensborough 2024 and LaGuardia 2024. Other campuses are not yet verified. Homelessness, housing insecurity, and rent difficulty are distinct, overlapping measures; do not add them together or multiply these rates by enrollment to invent counts.

Support links: campus resource descriptions are starting points, not confirmed service availability. No student identities or residence locations are mapped.
